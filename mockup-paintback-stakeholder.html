Why the redaction notice is visible, not hidden. An invisible strip looks like an omission. A visible notice says "this is deliberate, this is the discipline, this is what protects you." Sponsors learn quickly that the Strategist captures the political subtext on the blackboard for its own engagement planning but never lets it leak into rendered artefacts. The trust is built by showing the discipline, not by hiding the fact that something has been removed.
Paint-back conventions
Four connected frames
Power-interest grid · RACI matrix · governance forums · communication plan. Each renders as a separate frame on the same canvas section. Stakeholders appear once in the grid; their disposition shows as a coloured left-stripe on the tile.
Disposition layered on grid
Power-interest position determines engagement approach (manage closely / keep satisfied / etc.). Disposition (champion / supportive / neutral / sceptical) determines what each stakeholder needs to actively support the programme. Both are visible on the same tile.
RACI for load-bearing decisions only
The matrix shows the programme-defining decisions (Gate A, Gate B, business case approval) — not standing org RACI. One Accountable per decision; multiple As means the framing is wrong and gets flagged.
Political signals are absent — and the absence is visible
The redaction notice is rendered, not hidden. Sponsors see the discipline working. Captured content lives in framing_notes.political_signals on the blackboard; the render function strips it. Verified at render time, not by convention.
