Why this is the primary surface. A capability heat map is the kind of artefact people screenshot and share. The grid format is dense, scannable, and emotionally resonant — red cells draw attention without explanation. Renders well on canvas, renders badly on slides (the deck version is a summary, not the full grid). Mladen's paint-back routine for this output is more design-sensitive than any of the previous three because the canvas is the deliverable.
Paint-back conventions
Three connected frames
Process pain (left) · capability heat map (centre, largest) · system landscape (right). The heat map is the load-bearing frame; the side frames provide context.
Signal vs finding language
Pain cards say "Root-cause signal" not "Root cause." The strategist surfaces signals; proving root cause is the Analyst-Compiler's job in Stage 2. This discipline lives in the labels.
Heat map colours mean specific things
Coverage: green/yellow/red for full/partial/none. Maturity: gradient by score 1–5 (darker = lower). Pain: red/orange/pink for high/medium/low. Capability name + Salesforce reference both visible — mismatches flagged.
Framing notes painted inline
Scope_overflow and unverified_signals get their own zone above the comments — visible to sponsors during review without requiring a separate appendix. The agent does not silently absorb these into the assessment.
Two interaction modes
Default review mode — sponsors add comment stickies, comments don't mutate the schema. Optional workshop iteration mode — specific cells or cards become directly editable. Mode set per session by the facilitator.
